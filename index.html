01 / Instrument

The Muzelle

Behind the glass

Two fans of pale glass meet above the keyboard of KV Hopper’s upright. Slide the doors apart and a second way to play the instrument comes into view. A paper roll travels across a narrow reader while the keys move below it.

The brass plate gives the piano a name. The plate inside carries serial number 114658. Its model and assembly date remain open questions. The closest published records connect the Muzelle name to two places: a piano factory in South Korea and a mechanical-music workshop in Las Vegas.

A piano and a player

In a technician’s account of a 1988 Muzelle DW-7, the underlying upright is attributed to Sojin, part of Daewoo in South Korea. Inside that example, mallets, a xylophone and a tambourine extend the piano into a small mechanical ensemble. A coin slot starts the performance. Hamilton Piano Company documents a second example, a black DW-7A dated 1989. These model numbers belong to those individual pianos.

The Las Vegas connection

In 2013, Barry Casper identified himself as the builder of the Muzelle nickelodeon line. Collector Rob Goodale recalled Muzelle as the house brand of Music World, a Las Vegas retailer owned by Don and Dorothy Kemp. Dorothy made decorative glass for the instruments. A panel on the DeBence Museum’s Muzelle was reported to carry her name and a June 1991 date. The glass on Hopper’s piano offers a visual connection, though its maker remains unidentified.

Reading the roll

On a pneumatic player, the paper normally covers the openings in the tracker bar. A perforation passing over an opening admits air into a small tube. A valve switches the corresponding pneumatic to suction. As its bellows folds, a linkage operates the piano action and the hammer strikes a string. The paper stores timing and pitch. The piano supplies the sound.

These illustrated references explain the mechanism. A factory service manual matched to Hopper’s instrument has yet to surface.
